# Pipeline

Owns how a source PDF becomes a final sheet. The reader row is provisional until the comparison in `muse-spike` measures it.

| Stage | Pick | State | Disproved by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Read notes | Direct reader of PDF symbols, 1 decoder per encoding | locked | a bar disagreeing with the source when read by eye |
| Cross-check | homr on every source page | locked | a real disagreement left unresolved |
| Read chords and text | Direct PDF text extraction | locked | a chord or cue missing from the output |
| Write | MusicXML with explicit system breaks | locked | a wrong import in MuseScore |
| Transpose | MuseScore `--transpose` | locked | a verifier mismatch on notes, chords or key |
| Export | MuseScore `mscore` with a fixed style file | locked | a header, footer or banned text in the PDF |

## Source encodings
- Know every music glyph an orchestral source prints: the dynamics letters, the accidentals inside text and the tuplet digits of its text font; tenuto, marcato, accent below, up-bow, down-bow, harmonic circle, bass clef, alto clef and the time digit of its music font; the augmentation dot, system bracket ends, wavy arpeggio line, treble-8vb clef, diamond notehead and tremolo slashes of its 3rd font. Why: an undecoded glyph is refused, never skipped.
- Decode MuseScore-exported lead sheets through a Leland decoder reading vector glyphs, stems and beams, one more entry in the 1-per-encoding table, with no new dependency. Why: the music glyphs are standard SMuFL characters, and a page image adds only error.
- Find a staff line in every decoder that reads drawn staves as the longest run of touching strokes over 100pt at 1 height, never a single long stroke. Why: an export draws some staves 1 bar at a time, and a stack of short ledger lines touches nowhere.
- Group a lower staff into the system above when every bar line it has falls on the upper staff's and either both end together or a bar line runs down from the upper staff into it. Why: a part entering later starts its staff late, and a part leaving early ends it soon.
- Write each key signature with the mode the source's file name declares, minor for a name ending in m and major otherwise, and refuse to write a key whose mode is unset. Why: a major song written as minor carries a false key in the owner's file.
- Give every decoded mark, text or hairpin, to exactly 1 line in every decoder, bounding each line's search window by its neighbours' shared midpoint. Why: a mark between 2 lines read by both doubles it.
- Give each Leland text to 1 line: the line whose window holds it, and where 2 windows overlap, by what it is: a lyric hint, set in the source's lyric font (FreeSerif) under its staff, to the line, and on a 2-staff line to the staff, it sits under; a chord, cue, mark or ending, printed above its staff, to the line whose staff it sits above. Why: a text read by both lines doubles, distance cannot part a lyric under 1 staff from a cue stacked over the next line's chords, cues set partly in plain Times share no style with lyrics, and a lyric filed under the next line puts a performer in a bar early.
- Read any bold label printed in a box as a section mark, whatever its font, and give it to the bar whose opening barline lies under its box or within a few points of it, nearest its middle, since an engraver sets a section box on, just before or just after the barline it marks, else to the bar it sits in; a symbol-font source sets every mark at the start of a line beside its printed bar number, read as that bar. Why: an engraver centres a section box on the barline it marks, so it starts inside the bar before, and a performer coming in at A2 or the interlude a bar early is lost.
- Set every edit the extension makes from the score's own state, never add it to what an earlier run left: a run of the extension on its own output saves the same score, proven on stacked cues over 3 more runs. Why: the extension runs many times per sheet, and any edit that adds up moves the sheet a little further each run.
- Raise each text above its line's chords from where it stood before any raising: every run of the extension first undoes the lift it recorded in the score, lays the score out again, then measures and records the new lift. Why: the extension runs many times per sheet, and lifts that stack run after run push a line's texts 60 staff spaces up and its staff a quarter of the page down.
- Refuse a page that leaves a blank band taller than 15% of its height between its first and last ink, in the layout's final checks and in the gate. Why: whatever lifts a text or reserves room above a line, the symptom is the same empty band a reader sees, and a check on the band catches every cause of it.
- Write no empty text: a tempo printed without words writes its metronome mark alone, and the gate refuses a score file holding a text with nothing to print. Why: MuseScore still places an empty text, the raising pass lifts it above every chord, and the room it reserves pushes the first line down a quarter of the page.
- Give each text to the bar it is printed under, and a text that starts within a few points before a barline and runs across it to the bar after it, as for a chord. Why: an engraver starts a lyric a hair left of the barline it begins at, and a performer comes in a bar early when the sheet files it a bar late.
- Give a Leland repeat dot to the bar it sits inside, opening that bar's repeat near its left edge and closing it near its right. Why: where a key change parts a double barline from a start repeat, the nearest edge is the bar before, and the start reads as an end.
- Hold every Leland source's decoded line starts equal to the bar numbers it prints at the left of each line. Why: a dropped or split staff shifts every later bar while each bar still reads.
- Keep a mid-song key change in the score and move it by the song's own interval. Why: the change belongs to the song.

## Orchestral score
- Give each word, dynamic, mark and hairpin of an orchestral page to exactly 1 system: within its reach of that system's staves and on its side of the halfway line to each neighbouring system. Why: 2 fixed reaches overlap wherever systems print close, and a glyph in both is read twice or by the wrong part.
- Give a clef only to the staff it stands on, within that staff's own lines and ledger reach, never to the nearest staff of a system it does not belong to. Why: a clef of the next system read as a change on this system's bottom staff puts its notes 46 octaves off.
- Port an orchestral source as every staff printed, in its original key, with transposing instruments kept, to the 2 MuseScore files and 1 PDF only. Why: the file is the whole score, and the piano sheet and favourite tone do not apply.
- Exempt an orchestral score from the size rule and the line-layout rules, keeping the source's system breaks. Why: a full score reads system by system.
- Print an orchestral score at the largest staff space, from 0.9 mm down in steps of 0.05 mm, at which every source system holds on 1 line, judged on MuseScore's own layout; refuse the score when none does. Why: a system MuseScore wraps puts a break the source never made into the middle of a phrase.
- Read an orchestral source from its own folder, `Orchestral` beside the lead-sheet folders, named `<name>-<concert key>.pdf` like every source. Why: the folder says which pipeline a source takes, and the name carries the key the gate checks.
- Name each staff of an orchestral system from the label printed beside it in that system, never by its order, and give a label centred between 2 braced staves to both. Why: a full score hides empty staves, so a system's staff count changes page to page, and a harp's 1 label stands between its 2 staves.
- Read an orchestral score as 1 event per stem with all its heads, each beamed figure on the staff its beam serves, voices apart only where 2 stems or heads coincide, flat beams as beams, double dots as 7/4, and a bracketed 3 or 5 scaling every stem and rest under it. Why: these are what a full score engraves, and each was measured on Thaïs, which then reads 1,215 of 1,219 staff-bars whole.
- Name every staff of an orchestral score: the full instrument name on the first system, its short name on every later one, the first system indented to hold them. Why: a player finds their staff by its name, and the lead-sheet style hides names a single-instrument sheet never needs.
- Carry every mark an orchestral score prints on a note to that note and write it with MusicXML's own element: tenuto, marcato above or below, up-bow, down-bow, harmonic, arpeggio and tremolo, beside the accent, fermata and trill. Why: a bowing or an articulation dropped from a part changes how it is played.
- Read a hairpin as 2 straight strokes meeting at 1 end and opening at the other, a crescendo where they meet on the left, and write it as a wedge from the note under its start to the note under its end. Why: a hairpin is drawn, never set as a glyph, so only its geometry names it.
- Spread an orchestral score's systems over the whole page height. Why: the source's width sets the size, and a system left at the top of an empty page reads as a broken print.
- Write a printed tempo of an orchestral score as its words and a metronome mark, its note drawn by the music font, never as a note character inside text. Why: the text font has no note, so the PDF falls back to a foreign font and the gate refuses it.
- Keep a harp line that crosses between its 2 staves as 1 line across them. Why: the engraver writes it without rests, so 2 separate staves would each read half a bar.

## Build cost
- Key every song's build and gate result on a digest of its source, the code and the style, and skip what is unchanged. Why: adding 1 song or fixing 1 sheet type must not rebuild the rest.
- Key every cache on what it was built from, never on a name or a path. Why: a name-keyed import from older code was reused and refused every layout.
- Store the embedded chord list by name in every published score's style, never by a build path. Why: a build path is machine-local and resolves nowhere else.
- Publish each song the moment it passes its gate, not at the end of a run. Why: a finished song owes the reader nothing further.
- Purge superseded sheet versions from the sheets repo's history once a build has published its last song, never after each song. Why: a purge rewrites the whole history, so 1 per song serialised every publish behind a mirror clone, a filter and an aggressive repack.
- Build each song in its own staging folder, copied into the sheets repo only under the publish lock. Why: publishing resets that repo, wiping any other song still building there.
- Run every render and every extension run as 1 request to a pool of the fork's `mscore --serve` processes, 1 per launch slot, each started once and kept running, and restart a process that dies or passes its deadline. Why: a launch costs about 1 second of startup while a served render costs about 0.2, and a served render and seating were proven to lay out exactly as a fresh launch's.
- Gather the renders many threads ask for into 1 request. Why: renders that share a request share its turn in the pool.
- Name the MuseScore every run uses in `MUSE_MSCORE`: a copy of the installed MuseScoreLite under its own bundle identifier, never the installed app itself, since macOS hands the owner's opened files to a running server of the same app; and refuse to start when it is unset or names no executable. Why: the official app on the path has no `--serve`, and a run that quietly picked it would fail mid-song.
- Run every MuseScore-driven test against `MUSE_MSCORE`, and fail rather than skip when it is unset. Why: a skip keyed on the path hid every extension test.
- Fail every render of a MuseScore job that raises, with the error as its reason, and keep the batcher serving the renders after it. Why: a job that raises past the batcher leaves it flushing, and every later render in that process waits for ever.
- Delete, after each MuseScore launch, every log file named for a second the launch ran in whose every conversion reads a file under the launch's own folder, and keep any other log, the owner's own sessions above all. Why: MuseScore writes a log per launch and never prunes, so 120 thousand logs from the pipeline made 8 simultaneous launches 2.2 times slower, and only a log whose conversions are all the pipeline's is the pipeline's to delete.
- Stop the whole run, naming the cause, when a tool fails because the locked Mac refuses it the session's devices: MuseScore aborting because the window server refuses it its pasteboard, or homr's CoreML model unable to compute a prediction, as both do while the Mac sits at the login or lock window; never refuse each song for it. Why: the fault is the machine's, every song would be refused for nothing, and the reason must say to unlock the Mac.
- Sample a MuseScore run's native stack the moment it passes its deadline, before it is killed, and name the sample file in the timeout's error. Why: a run that hangs inside MuseScore leaves no trace once killed, and a timeout without evidence is indistinguishable from load.
- Print every thread's stack when a build process receives SIGUSR1. Why: a hung build shows where it waits without a debugger that needs root.
- Fail fast in order of cost: preflight every song in seconds (decode, key, piano parts, tests, lint, control anchors), then the layout, then the gate as confirmation. Why: a defect visible in seconds must not wait hours.
- Stop a song at its first refusal and let other songs continue; refuse a chain whose tests or controls fail before any build. Why: 1 song must not block the rest, and a failed check must never run on.
- Start a heavy launch only while the CPU measured idle over the last second leaves a whole core free, 1 reading per second shared by every launch, never 1 blocking second per launch, and wait for that with a bound that names the idle share it gave up on; never judge it by the load average. Why: the cores are shared with other work, a run started on a saturated machine dies at its deadline while doing nothing wrong, and the load average counts waiting threads, reading 19 on 10 cores with 82% of the CPU idle.
- Start 1 song worker per song up to the core count, split the cores among them as launch slots so heavy launches never exceed the cores, and report build time per song. Why: a worker count read from 1 load sample stays fixed for the whole build, and a load spike at start runs every song in series on an idle machine.
- Lay out a song's original, favourite-tone and piano sheets at once, each in its own thread. Why: they share nothing but the build record, and renders asked for at the same time share 1 MuseScore launch.
- Raster a render's chord ink once per first letter and size, from the first chord no other glyph crowds, and place every later chord of that letter and size by the same offset past its text box. Why: within 1 letter and size the offset varies by under 0.1 points, below the 0.4-point tolerance, and rastering each chord was most of a hard song's build.
- Read each rendered PDF's chord lines once per version of the file. Why: 1 render was read 2 to 3 times by the checks that follow it.
- Read a chord's ink from 1 render of its line's band, and find the characters near it by height in 1 sorted index of the page. Why: 1 render per chord and 1 pass over every character per chord made reading ink a third of a song's build.
- Import each sheet once, with a style at no particular size, and give every candidate size an edited copy. Why: the import does not depend on size, and 1 import per size multiplied launches by the number of sizes.
- Gate each song in its own child process when publishing, on its staged folder, and record a pass by each file's name within its song folder, so the publishing process finds the pass for the same bytes in the sheets repo; check the chord names, which every song shares, once per process. Why: a gate run by the 1 publishing process serialised every song's 20 to 220 s gate.
- Build each song in its own child process, its report written to a file the parent reads, so a free slot takes the next song; publish 1 song at a time from the parent, and run planted-bug controls in parallel. Why: a fixed batch waits on its slowest song, publishing rewrites shared history, and a process pool's shared result queue stalled with 10 workers while every song had finished.
- Stop a run the moment its collector ends early, whatever ended it: print the cause at once and kill every running song worker with every process it started. Why: a collector that stops while workers run otherwise waits hours for them, publishing nothing and saying nothing.
- Build songs in the owner's priority order, kept in private `muse-spike`, then the rest by name, and publish from the 1 process that collects finished songs. Why: the songs wanted first arrive first, and 1 collector serialises publishing without a lock file.
- Prove every pipeline change end to end on 1 song before a full run, and never change the code a running build loads. Why: each untested change landed mid-run and cost hours of builds.

## MuseScore Studio exports
- Read a staff as a second staff WHEN its bar lines fall where the staff above has them. Why: some lines print an extra part and others do not.
- Read a mid-song key or time change from signs beside no note. Why: these songs change key late.
- Read 2 segno signs as target then jump, and 2 coda signs as jump then target. Why: these sheets print bare signs without words.
- Cross-check every export with homr, as every other source. Why: a vector read and an image read share no failure.

## Song file
- Extract each source once into 1 JSON song file in private `muse-spike`, holding musical facts such as beats and no PDF positions, verified at extraction. Why: every later stage then needs only this file, so a decoder change rebuilds nothing.
- Apply a correction by editing the song file. Why: a fix to the music has 1 home and its own history.
- Key a build on the song file, the style and the code that can shape a sheet, derived as the import closure of the modules that write it and the extension it installs, never a hand-kept list of exclusions. Why: a change that cannot alter a sheet must not rebuild it, and a hand list rots: it keyed sheets on the status reporter while excluding a module every sheet had come to use.

## Calculated layout
- Measure each sheet once per candidate size, all sizes in 1 MuseScore job, with the page so wide and the last line so unjustified that every bar prints at its natural width, and run the extension on that render in its measure mode, which writes back through a score tag the share each bar must grow to hold its chords apart by the clear gap; a beat-1 chord's need is its distance from the barline, past any sign there, plus its own room, before the next chord's share of the bar, while a later chord sits at a share of its bar. Why: natural widths and chord needs predict line breaks, so lines are planned in code rather than found by trial renders, and a sign's fixed room read as a share asks a narrow bar to grow a hundredfold.
- Balance a sheet's lines over its pages from the final render, after seating and after every line-gap change, and lay it out again until the page breaks hold; refuse a size whose printed pages differ from the balanced split of their own line heights. Why: seating, raised texts and a wider line gap make lines taller after the first balance, and a line pushed past the page's end lands alone on a page of its own. The gate judges every published PDF the same way, from the line starts its own bar numbers print.
- Judge a sheet's line rules in the layout's final checks with the gate's own functions, the tie over a line end and, in strict mode, even empty bars among them, so a size the gate would refuse is refused there and the next size tried. Why: a rule only the gate knows blocks the whole song after its size was chosen.
- Plan each section's line breaks in code as 1 optimal search over every break: fewest lines first, then the most even fill to 93% of a line's width, with a cost on a lone bar and on a 2-bar line, never a break after a tied bar, never a line opening on a bar without a chord unless the source itself opens a line on that chordless bar, and on a piano sheet a mark bar joined to the label block after it; budget each bar at its natural width grown by its chord share and each empty bar at the widest empty bar of its line, and choose the size from the plan. Why: every line rule holds by construction, where greedy packing needed a repair for each rule, and code decides in milliseconds; a chordless bar the engraver chose to open a line on reads as the original does.
- Judge a line's width as the sum of each bar's own chord-widened width, its empty bars evened to the widest of them, in both modes. Why: the empty bars of a line widen together to hold their chords, so the plan budgets what prints.
- Change line starts only through the plan: when a render shows a line wrapped, or after seating still holds chords closer than the clear gap or ink past the margin, grow the width estimate of that line's bars and plan again (for a crowded line, only its bars whose own chords need more room than their notes give, when it has any), so every line rule holds on every pass; text overlapping text is never a width fault, and the final checks refuse it with its own reason. Why: moving 1 bar by hand strands lone bars that no rule sees, and growing a dense bar for its neighbour's chords inflates it until it stands alone.
- Finish a sheet's candidate sizes 1 at a time, in their order, skip a size not yet started, and stop a running size at its next plan, once its lines cannot beat the best finished, and keep the fewest finished lines with ties to the earlier size. Why: songs already fill every core, so sizes finished side by side only add work the stop would have spared; measured on all 25 songs, 113 sizes finished instead of 193 and the run took 2,126 s instead of 2,466 s with every sheet identical.
- Keep replanning a size while its overflowing bars still reach a new low, and refuse it only after 6 plans in a row bring none, or when no untried plan remains, naming which. Why: a fixed count of plans refused a sheet that went from 30 overflowing bars to 3 and was still falling, so it printed a size smaller than it needed.
- Turn off automatic placement for every chord symbol and jump sign. Why: placed chords then never set a bar's width, and nothing moves what code placed.
- Fit and seat chords inside MuseScore with the pipeline's own extension, run through `--extension`, laying the score out again after each change in the same process: it widens through `userStretch` each bar whose chords lack the clear gap to the next chord, never a bar alone on its line, which already spans the line, makes no beat-1 chord correction farther than 10 staff spaces, a bound no measured chord nears, leaving such a chord to the final checks, evens a line's empty bars to its widest, moves every beat-1 chord to its barline or line start, starts a line's first chord after any sign there, lifts each line's chords on 1 baseline only as far as its notes, tuplet brackets, slurs, ties and voltas require, every slur and tie wherever it sits and a volta or other spanner only within 10 staff spaces of its own line, since MuseScore never moves a volta clear of chords placed by hand and its number otherwise prints on them, never over the clef and signatures opening the line, which no chord sits over, and raises any cue, mark or tempo the chord row would touch to sit above the chords and above any text already raised over the same span, so texts stack rather than overprint. Why: 1 launch measures and places what render-and-parse took dozens of launches to approach.
- Even a line's empty bars from each fresh render, toward the line's mean in both directions, with the gate's own measure of a bar: after the clef, key and time signature, never after a sign, which moves only where the first chord starts. Why: the extension's own layout differs from the print, and evening from it left bars several points apart.
- Leave a bar's width to its notes and the evening, never to its chord text: a line whose chords still crowd, or print merged, goes back to the plan for fewer bars. Why: chord text never sets a bar's width, on any sheet.
- After the seating rounds, measure the latest render and correct it until every chord sits on target and every line's empty bars pass the final checks' own evenness judgement; end on a measured render that matches the file, never on a correction left unrendered. Judge no render older than its score file: wherever a render is measured or returned, a file written after it is rendered again first. Why: a stretch set after the last seating moves chords off their barlines, and a file changed after its last render is judged on a print that is not its own.
- Judge every seating round on a fresh render of the saved score, never on a PDF the extension writes from inside its own launch. Why: the layout inside the launch differs from the one MuseScore gives the saved file, so a round judged in-launch reads seated while the printed chord sits 3 spaces off.
- Refuse a sign box crossing a barline into a bar the score holds no target in, and grow the reach by how far it crosses, as for a box over a word. Why: a jump's box over the next bar breaks its own rule even where no chord sits under it to report it.
- Grow the reach a sign's box keeps cues from, passed to the extension as a score tag, by the overlap a fresh render measures between any sign box and a word, and seat again until none remains. Why: the extension reads a cue beside a line-opening sign several points from where it prints, and a wider line gap never parts 2 things on 1 line.
- Move a sign the extension shifts sideways only after taking it out of automatic placement at the very height automatic placement gave it. Why: moved with automatic placement on, it stacks above any cue it now meets, and a fixed height lands it on chords.
- Measure where a sign stops taking room 1 way for both the chord seating and the touch check: the further of its drawn box and its glyph's printed extent, with a clearance that holds a chord letter's side bearing. Why: 2 definitions put a chord exactly where 1 check wants and the other refuses, and every size then fails.
- Seat a chord after or before a sign by the sign's drawn box alone, never by the grown reach, and leave the rest to the measured chord corrections. Why: the reach grows for any word a box still covers anywhere, and chords placed by it drift 30 points past their target.
- Pass the clear gap in staff spaces, and each chord's measured offset between its text box and its ink, to the extension through score tags, and run it again until the ink sits on target. Why: the gap has 1 home in the code, and the extension sees text boxes while the gate judges ink.
- Read a chord's ink from its own letters alone: black ink only, and where any other character's box enters the window, the side bearing its first letter shows on the PDF's uncrowded chords. Why: a key signature's sharp beside a chord set low, or a line-opening sign's red or blue box, reads as the chord's first ink, and a music font's boxes are too tall to mask it, so every correction chases a phantom.
- Scale each chord's next correction by how far its last correction actually moved its ink, once a round has measured that response. Why: a chord whose ink answers a correction more or less than asked overshoots and flips side every round.
- Add a measured offset to a chord's correction only within 5 staff spaces, and ignore a larger one. Why: a line's opening read by the extension and by the check differs by up to about 3 spaces, while a chord paired with the wrong score chord sits a chord's distance off, and feeding that back pushes it onto its neighbour.
- Report a chord the score holds and the print lacks as a final-check problem that refuses the size, never as an error that ends the song. Why: a chord pushed off the page is 1 layout's fault, and the next size can hold it.
- Widen the gap between lines in steps and lay the sheet out again when its final checks find text on text, a sign's box over a word included, up to a gap 20 staff spaces past the base, before refusing the size; never stop after a fixed number of steps. Why: chords placed by code take no room MuseScore reserves, so text below 1 line can reach the next line's chords.
- Count as text on text a cue below 1 line and a cue above the next that share a stretch of the page closer than 2 staff spaces, each owned by its line through the score's own placement, never guessed from the page. Why: 2 cues that close at 1 height read as 1 doubled cue, and only the score says which line each belongs to.
- Tie a printed span to a cue only when its words are whole, consecutive words of that cue, and keep a span that fits cues of both lines on both sides, so a cue printed on both lines is still found beside its twin. Why: a substring tie let a chord's letter `A` count as a word of `BASS IN`, so a line's own cue read as crowding the line above at every line gap, and each such size widened the gap 10 times before refusing.
- Refuse a size whose fitting plan breaks a line rule before seating it, never after. Why: seating is the dearest step and a broken line rule refuses the size whatever seating does; measured on all 25 songs every chosen sheet stays identical, and the slowest song alone drops from 619 s to 468 s.
- Judge crowding, chord collisions and text on text only on a seated layout, never on a plan or a wrap correction. Why: unseated chords collide by construction, so an earlier check refuses every layout.
- Fail a measurement that cannot read what it measures, never return a neutral value in its place. Why: a zero chord need packed 12 bars on 1 line and read as a seating fault.
- Install the extension from the code repo into MuseScoreLite's extensions folder in `~/mslite/extensions` under a name and address carrying a digest of its own bytes, and run it by that address. Why: a build must not depend on a hand-installed copy, and 2 runs of different versions, such as planted-bug controls, must never share 1 installed copy.

- Never carry a bar's learned width growth from 1 candidate size to another. Cost: measured on all 25 songs it halved the build time but printed 13 sheets longer and 3 shorter, because a bar that grows at 1 size is widened at sizes where it fit.

## Measured MuseScore behaviour
- Rewrite every Expression an import produces as staff text with the same placement, before any layout. Why: MuseScore imports italic-only words as an Expression and sets all expressions under a staff on 1 row without avoiding each other, so a long cue overprints a later one.
- MuseScore needs the login session's window server: launched from a shell outside it, such as a terminal multiplexer whose server outlived a restart, it aborts on every file with `PasteBoard: Error creating pasteboard ... [-4960]`, and even `open -a` refuses there with `kLSNoExecutableErr`, so a build runs only from a shell started inside the logged-in session.
- `mscore -j` loads every score of a job file in 1 process; an entry takes a list of outputs and a `transpose` object, and upstream keeps nothing running between jobs; the fork's `--serve` reads 1 job per line of standard input and stays running.
- `mscore --extension <uri>` runs an installed extension on each loaded score before it is written; the extension reads every element's page position and box, moves offsets, and sees the layout redone after `endCmd`.
- An extension receives the plugin API's score functions and no process API: `writeScore` exports only the score it was launched with, `readScore` with its noninteractive flag is not implemented and returns nothing, and `newQProcess` is undefined; 1 extension run therefore serves 1 score, and upstream offers no way to keep 1 process serving many.
- The layout an extension sees after its own changes differs from the layout of the same saved score on a fresh load: a chord seated in the launch can print several spaces elsewhere, so only a fresh render is evidence.
- A chord symbol with automatic placement widens its bar to fit its name, whatever `minHarmonyDistance` and `harmonyHarmonyDistance` say; with automatic placement off it never does, and nothing lifts it clear of cues, brackets or voltas.
- A page 200 inches wide with `lastSystemFillLimit` 1 prints a song on 1 line at natural bar widths; a page of ordinary width stretches every bar to fill its line.
- A bar's `stretch` only widens it: a bar a repeat barline or another fixed element holds wide never narrows.
- A score tag (`metaTag`) carries values both ways between the pipeline and its extension: `curScore.metaTag` reads 1 and `setMetaTag` writes 1 into the saved file.
- A bar's `userStretch` is writable from the extension and lays out again after `endCmd`.
- Automatic placement widens a bar for its chords with less than the clear gap: a natural render with it on still printed 29 chord collisions, so the pipeline sets chord room itself.
- Chords that overlap in a render merge into 1 text span in the PDF, so chord names are never counted from the spans of a render whose chords can overlap.
- The extension sees a chord's text box and the gate judges its ink; the 2 differ by up to about 0.5 point a chord, so each chord's offset is measured after 1 run and passed back.
- Some chords at a line start move several times the distance their offset asks, so a correction computed from 1 fixed scale overshoots.
- Lines are grouped by page and height; grouping by height alone merges the first lines of 2 pages.
- A line's first chord starts after the clef, the key and any sign printed there; a start-repeat barline does not move it.

## Class mechanisms
- Measure a print only beside positions drawn in the same load of its score: draw a PDF and its measure positions in 1 job, keep a published PDF's positions stamped with that PDF's digest, and let the gate refuse a PDF whose stamp does not match rather than render it again. Why: 2 renders of 1 file place bars up to a point apart, which accuses correct sheets.
- Judge a sheet in the layout with the gate's own checks: 1 function per rule (line rules, paper size, ink at the edge, short-line share, page balance) that both call, the layout on its plan and its final render, the gate on the published file. Why: a rule only the gate knows lets the layout finish a sheet the gate then refuses, and 2 copies of 1 rule drift.
- Read which rows and columns of a page hold ink through 1 reader, at 1 ink level, and refuse a blank page wherever a line's height is read from its ink. Why: 4 copies of 1 reading drift in threshold and stride, and a blank page has no first ink row.
- Build for the newest release of every app, tool and dependency the machine runs, and write no version number in any doc or code: nothing names, pins or branches on a version. Why: every app, the code and its dependencies are kept at their newest release, so a written version is stale the day it lands.
- Define each fact once: SMuFL code points and geometry shared by readers live in 1 module, and a test refuses a constant name defined in 2 modules unless it is listed as 2 distinct facts. Why: 2 copies of 1 fact drift apart silently.
- Compare every word in a source's text layer with the decoded score in preflight, excluding only credit words, chord fragments and jump words. Why: a lost cue passes every check built on the decoder's own reading.
- Check every planted-bug anchor in preflight, in seconds, before any control or build runs. Why: a stale control must not stop a chain late.
- Refuse a push on the whole suite, leaving out only the source tests when the private inputs are not named, never a hand-picked list of test files. Why: a list of 2 files let a broken layout test reach the remote while every named file passed.
- Run the fast suite a 2nd time in print mode on every push, and let every test that depends on the mode state which mode's rule it asserts. Why: a mode no build uses rots unseen, and a test reading the mode from the environment changes verdict with the machine.
- Mark every test that drives MuseScore itself, and run a planted-bug control against the fast tests alone unless it plants in code only MuseScore runs. Why: the MuseScore tests take minutes, and every control paying for them made the controls take hours.
- Refuse ink past any edge of the page in the layout's own checks, not only past the right margin. Why: text raised above the first line left the page at the top and passed every vector check.
- Tie a saved layout to a digest of the layout code and style, never a hand-raised number. Why: a forgotten bump republishes a stale sheet.
- Write a trial build's records to its own folder named in `MUSE_BUILD_STATE`, never the published sheets' records. Why: a trial into a scratch sheets repo otherwise rewrites the records `muse.status` judges the published sheets by.
- Report published state only from `muse.status`, which reads the private repo's HEAD and the build records, judging each record against the key its own writer derives and only the files it delivers into a song's folder. Why: a state recalled from a run is not a state checked.

## Dependencies
- PyMuPDF: reads every glyph with its code, font and position, every vector path, and renders pages for the ink checks. Its required layout engine (`pymupdf-layout`, `pymupdf4llm`, onnxruntime and their support packages) serves nothing here and holds Python at the newest release onnxruntime ships for; no build of PyMuPDF without it exists. Rejected: pypdfium2, measured on all 25 sources: its text layer drops 444 printed glyphs, 92% of them within 6 points of an identical glyph, which in music are real stacked noteheads and repeated beam and flag glyphs, and it offers no other per-glyph position; pdfplumber, built on `pdfminer.six`, whose releases fall outside the maintenance window; poppler text output, which has no path data. Maintenance: active.
- MuseScoreLite, the owner's build of MuseScore 4 from the sibling repos `mslite` (code) and `mslite-doc` (docs): upstream's newest release plus the `--serve` mode the pipeline runs on, keeping the extension runner and the PDF, measure position, MIDI, MusicXML and MuseScore file exports the pipeline asks for; import, transposition, export, and every file the pipeline publishes. Maintenance: active.
- homr: independent cross-check only. Rejected as reader: returns no chords or text. Maintenance: active.
- Rejected: Audiveris, 53 of 62 bars exact on the comparison page and no chords. Rejected: oemer, unmaintained. Rejected: PDFtoMusic Pro, paid and unneeded.

- psutil: measures idle CPU for the launch gate. Rejected: the load average, which counts waiting threads; parsing `top`, a hand-roll of what psutil owns. Maintenance: its repository commits each week and it is the standard Python reader of system load, though releases are far apart; trigger to revisit: the repository is archived.
- Python and every dependency at their newest releases, resolved fresh from the manifest by `setup.sh`, which takes the newest Python every dependency installs on; no version is written down or pinned anywhere. Why: the machine updates every app often, and a written version only goes stale.
- pytest: tests in `muse`. Maintenance: active.
- lintmax-py (`1qh/lintmax-py`): the one gate for every Python, shell and other file in `muse`, run as `lintmax-py fix` and `lintmax-py check`, with no rule configured in the project: every ruff rule, ty at error, dead code, unused dependencies, vulnerabilities, spelling, shell scripts. Rejected: a hand-picked ruff rule set with mypy, which misses most of what the gate catches.
- git-filter-repo: strips superseded sheet versions from the sheets repo's history by blob. Rejected: BFG, which deletes by file name and so empties every earlier commit of current sheets too. Maintenance: its repository stays active and Git's own documentation recommends it for history rewrites, though releases are far apart; trigger to revisit: the repository is archived or Git ships the capability itself.

## Mechanism
- MuseScore 4 import keeps jump words and signs as staff text, so the port rewrites them into jump elements and markers carrying `<sym>`, `markerType`, `markerCenterOnSymbol` and `musicSymbolSize`. An untyped marker prints at the end of its bar.
- A chord kind is written as `other` with its text, because a standard kind drops extensions on import.
- Print chords through the pipeline's own chord description, kept in the code repo, which renders the circle and triangle as SF Pro letters and a slash chord's bass level with its root. Why: MuseScore 4 builds its own description into the app, so the file a sheet prints by must live with the code that writes it.

- Judge a beat-1 chord from where its bar's music opens: the barline, or the end of a key or time signature printed right after it, or past the box of a segno or coda whose middle lies just past that barline and in the bar's first half, as a target opening its bar does and a jump ending a narrow bar never does, mid-line as at a line's start, as the extension seats it; the signature run ends at the first note, however far above or below the staff its ledger lines put it, and is 1 tight run starting right at the barline, so a courtesy signature at a bar's far end opens nothing. Why: 2 definitions of 1 edge refuse a correctly seated chord.
- Judge chords against the measure boxes the score program exports with `.mpos`, reading only chord ink from the PDF. Why: a barline guessed from drawn strokes confuses stems, beams and short staves.

## Refusals
- Refuse an unknown music encoding by naming its fonts. Why: a wrong decoder reads confident garbage.
- Refuse an undecoded music symbol. Why: skipping it silently loses music.
- Refuse a missing SF Pro Display. Why: MuseScore otherwise falls back to another font without saying so.

## Gate
- Pass `muse.gate` before publishing: bars, pitches, chords, keys, navigation, inverse transposition, removed text, fonts, chord gap, margins, title, A4, ink edges, ties over line ends and a homr cross-check. Why: every rule lives in 1 command that states all failures at once.
- Check chord beats against the rule in `output-content.md`, and the built-in, boxed, wordless form of jump signs, on every MuseScore file built. Why: a chord in the right bar can still play late.
- Report a tie over a line end only where its tied run is too long for 1 line. Why: there the break is forced.
